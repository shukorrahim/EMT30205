Communication ProtocolsThe languages SCADA systems speak

Lecture notes by Mohamad Shukor bin Abdul Rahim

If SCADA is the brain of a plant, the communication network is its nervous system, and protocols are the rules the nerves follow. This chapter explains why protocols exist, how data is packed and checked, the OSI and TCP/IP models, Ethernet, and the protocols you will meet in industry: Modbus, IEC 60870-5, DNP3 and IEC 61850.

2.1Speaking the same language

For two devices to communicate, they must speak the same language (protocol) and follow the same rules for starting, conducting and ending the conversation. SCADA protocols grew out of the need to send data and control commands, locally and over long distances, in deterministic time.

Protokol: bahasa dan peraturan yang dipersetujui oleh dua peranti untuk berkomunikasi.

What is deterministic time?

The ability to predict how long a transaction will take when all relevant factors are known. A protection relay or an alarm cannot "maybe" arrive in time.

Think of a train and a taxi in KL traffic. The LRT leaves every 3 minutes and takes exactly 14 minutes: you can plan around it. A taxi might take 10 minutes or 50. SCADA needs the train.
Classroom check Why do you think early manufacturers made their protocols proprietary instead of open? Discuss for 60 seconds.
One answerCustomers who bought one brand had to keep buying that brand (vendor lock-in). It also let manufacturers tune the protocol tightly to their own hardware.

2.3Vendor protocols and open standards

To reach deterministic time in refineries and power utilities, PLC manufacturers developed their own protocols and bus structures. Today there are about 200 real-time protocols, proprietary and open. A big part of designing a SCADA system is matching protocols and communication settings between devices.

2.4The protocol envelope

A raw value like 12.5 means nothing on its own: 12.5 what? From which sensor? For which device? When a network is involved, the protocol adds source and destination addresses, a timestamp, control information and error checking around the raw data, following strict rules so the receiver can decode it.

Build the envelope one layer at a time, then send it.

Think of posting a parcel. You cannot drop a box in the post with no address. The protocol writes the "To" and "From" addresses on the box, stamps the time, and seals it with a check that shows if anything was damaged on the way.
Classroom check A system reads "12.5" but misses the addresses and units. What can happen? A) Nothing B) The system ignores it C) Catastrophic failure
AnswerC. The receiver might act on a value meant for another device, or in the wrong units. In a SCADA system with online control, the lecture notes warn that the result "can be disastrous".

2.5The OSI seven-layer model

The Open System Interconnection (OSI) reference model was issued by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) in 1984 and revised in 1994. It splits communication into seven layers, each with one clear job, so equipment from different vendors can work together. The sender packs the data going down the layers; the receiver unpacks it going up.

2.6TCP/IP: the model the Internet uses

Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) was developed at the same time as OSI. It is the model of the Internet, the biggest WAN in the world, and many SCADA protocols now run on it. TCP/IP has four layers: host-to-network (network access), Internet, transport and application. Tap a TCP/IP layer to see which OSI layers it covers.

SCADA: the app

The software that creates, reads and shows the messages, like WhatsApp on your phone.

TCP/IP: the road

The real four-layer network (Internet, Wi-Fi, Ethernet) that moves the packets from source to destination.

OSI: the law

The reference model that says how traffic must behave. Engineers use it to describe and troubleshoot networks.

2.7Ethernet

Ethernet is the network technology that connects devices inside a building. It uses only the physical and data link layers of the OSI model, and is defined by IEEE 802.3, which also lists the cable types, such as the RJ45 twisted-pair cable. Tap the timeline.

Sharing one cable: CSMA/CD

Early Ethernet put every device on one shared cable. The rule was carrier sense, multiple access with collision detection: listen before you talk; if two devices talk at once, both stop, send a jam signal, wait a random time, and try again. Try it, then compare with a modern switch.

    2.8Modbus

    Modbus was developed in 1979 by Modicon. It is free, open, and the most common protocol in SCADA and industrial control networks, used between master stations and RTUs. Modbus is an application layer protocol based on a master/slave architecture: one master controls all data transmission, and slaves only answer when asked to read or write data.

    Think of a strict teacher taking a roll call. The teacher (master) asks each student (slave) in turn, "Sensor 1, status?". No student may speak until called, even if something urgent happens.

    Think-pair-share What is the main drawback of master/slave polling if an emergency happens at sensor #50 while the master is polling sensor #2?
    AnswerThe master will not know until it reaches sensor #50 in its cycle. The worst-case delay grows with the number of slaves. Try it above: add slaves and press Emergency.

    Build a real Modbus RTU message

    Every Modbus RTU request is a short frame: slave address, function code, data, and a two-byte CRC error check. Change the fields and watch the bytes, and the CRC, update. These are the real bytes a SCADA master would send down an RS-485 cable.

    Modbus ASCII sends the same message as readable text characters. Modbus TCP drops the CRC (TCP checks errors) and adds a small header, and uses TCP port 502.

    2.9IEC 60870-5

    IEC 60870-5-101 is a standard for power system monitoring, control and the associated communications for telecontrol, teleprotection and telecommunications in electric power systems. It is a family of related standards. Tap each card.

    2.10DNP3: Distributed Network Protocol

    DNP3 was created by Westronic, Inc. (now GE Harris) in 1990. It is an open, intelligent, robust and efficient modern SCADA protocol, heavily used by electric and water utilities, and standardised as IEEE 1815.

    The big change: DNP3 outstations do not just wait to be asked. They report by exception: they send only what has changed, with an accurate timestamp, and can even send an alarm unsolicited.

    Think of a proactive student. Instead of waiting for the roll call, she raises her hand: "Excuse me, my temperature spiked 2 minutes ago, at 10:04:12."

      2.11IEC 61850 and GOOSE

      IEC 61850 started as "Communication networks and systems in substations": communication at substation level between relays, substation control and switchgear, so that devices from different vendors interoperate. It is a universal solution for a fully digital power utility automation system. The first edition has 14 parts (IEC 61850-x-y, 2002 to 2005).

      Banyak peranti boleh dihubungkan jika menggunakan protokol ini, termasuklah pengecas EV yang masih dalam pembangunan.

      Rewiring the substation

      Three levels, two buses

      One data model, three ways to talk

      IEC 61850 describes every device as standard objects that all vendors understand. Those objects are then sent in three ways, depending on how fast they must arrive.

      Client-server (MMS)

      Reports, settings and control from the station computer. Runs over TCP/IP. Reliable but not the fastest.

      GOOSE

      Generic Object Oriented Substation Event. Fast, peer-to-peer trip and interlock messages, sent straight onto Ethernet with priority tagging.

      Sampled Values

      Digitised current and voltage waveforms from merging units at the switchgear, thousands of samples per second, also straight onto Ethernet.

      GOOSE skips the coach

      With GOOSE, devices talk directly to each other without going through the SCADA master. A protection relay can tell a circuit breaker to trip within a few milliseconds. Press Fault! and watch both paths in slow motion.

      Quick poll True or false: IEC 61850 was designed to work over old, slow serial cables.
      AnswerFalse. IEC 61850 is Ethernet-native. It replaces bundles of copper wires with a high-speed digital LAN.

3.1One bit at a time

Key ideaSerial communication sends data one bit after another along a single wire. Parallel sends many bits at once, but needs many wires.

Computers store every letter and number as 8 bits (0s and 1s). To send it down a cable we have two choices: queue the bits up on one wire (serial), or give each bit its own wire (parallel). Type a letter and press Send.

↔ Swipe sideways to see the whole picture
Press Send to watch the letter travel.

Asynchronous: no shared clock

RS-232 is asynchronous: there is no clock wire telling the receiver when each bit arrives. Instead both sides agree on the speed beforehand (the baud rate, for example 9600 bits per second), and every character is wrapped in a start bit and a stop bit. The timing between bits is fixed, but the gap between characters can be any length.

Analogy: knocking before you speakThe start bit is a knock on the door: "listen, a letter is coming." The receiver then counts the next 8 bits at the agreed speed. The stop bit is "done". The next knock can come straight away or a minute later. (Bit mula ialah ketukan pada pintu: "dengar, satu huruf akan datang." Penerima kemudian mengira 8 bit seterusnya pada kelajuan yang dipersetujui. Bit henti bermaksud "selesai". Ketukan seterusnya boleh datang serta-merta atau seminit kemudian.)

Why serial survives in industry

Simple

Often just two wires. Cheap and easy to install for basic sensors and meters.

Tough

RS-422 and RS-485 use differential signals that shrug off the electrical noise of a factory floor (3.5).

Everywhere

Decades of PLCs, drives, meters and RTUs already speak serial, and they keep running for 20 years or more.

Recap: a protocol is the agreed language of a conversation (syntax, meaning and timing). That was Chapter 2.1. This chapter is about the road underneath it.

3.2Nodes and addresses

Key ideaA node is any device on a network that can send, receive or process data: a PLC, an HMI, an RTU or a computer. Each node has its own address.
Analogy: houses on a streetThe cable is the street and every node is a house with a house number. The postman walks past every house, but only the house whose number is on the envelope opens it. (Kabel ialah jalan, dan setiap nod ialah sebuah rumah yang mempunyai nombor rumah. Posmen berjalan melalui setiap rumah, tetapi hanya rumah yang nombornya tertulis pada sampul surat sahaja yang membukanya.)

3.3Half duplex and full duplex

Key ideaHalf duplex: data goes both ways, but only one way at a time. Full duplex: both sides can send at the same time.
Half duplex: a walkie-talkiePress to talk, say "over", then listen. If both press at once, nobody hears anything. RS-485 (two wires) works like this. (Tekan untuk bercakap, sebut "over", kemudian dengar. Jika kedua-dua pihak menekan serentak, tiada siapa yang dapat mendengar. RS-485 dua wayar berfungsi seperti ini.)
Full duplex: a telephone callBoth people can talk and listen at the same time, because there is a separate path each way. RS-232 and RS-422 work like this. (Kedua-dua pihak boleh bercakap dan mendengar pada masa yang sama kerana ada laluan berasingan bagi setiap arah. RS-232 dan RS-422 berfungsi seperti ini.)

3.4RS-232: the pioneer

Key ideaRS-232 connects exactly two devices over a short cable. It is simple, full duplex and asynchronous, but sensitive to noise.

RS stands for Recommended Standard. RS-232C (the "C" means the third version) was approved by the Electronic Industries Alliance (EIA) for connecting serial devices. Old PCs had RS-232 ports called COM1 and COM2; today we use a USB-to-serial adapter.

The DB9 connector: tap a pin

Tap a pinEach of the 9 pins has one job. The three gold ones, 2, 3 and 5, are enough for most PLC links.

Mouth to ear: the crossover

One device's transmit pin (TXD, the mouth) must reach the other device's receive pin (RXD, the ear). So the two data wires cross over. Ground goes straight across.

Why only 15 metres? Try it

RS-232 is unbalanced: one data wire, measured against a shared ground. Electrical noise from motors and welders adds straight onto that wire, and a longer cable picks up more of it. Drag the cable length and watch the received bits.

Analogy: talking to a friend in a noisy factoryStanding close, you hear them fine. Walk 50 metres away while a machine roars, and words get lost. RS-232 is one voice against the noise, so keep it short. (Apabila berdiri dekat, anda boleh mendengar kawan anda dengan jelas. Jika anda berjalan 50 meter jauhnya ketika mesin sedang bising, ada perkataan yang hilang. RS-232 ialah satu suara melawan bunyi bising, jadi kabelnya mesti pendek.)

Typical uses: connecting a PC to a PLC for programming (like the CX-Programmer link in Lab 1), a PC to a microcontroller, a modem or printer, and debugging industrial devices on the bench.

RS-232, RS-422 and RS-485 describe only the wires, plugs and voltages. In the OSI model that is layer 1, the physical layer (Chapter 2.5).

3.5RS-422 and the differential trick

Key ideaRS-422 sends each signal on a pair of wires carrying opposite copies. The receiver looks only at the difference between them, so noise that hits both wires cancels out.
Analogy: two friends on a bumpy busWhen the bus hits a pothole, both friends bounce up together. The gap between their heads stays the same. The receiver measures that gap, not the height, so the bumps (the noise) do not matter. (Apabila bas terlanggar lubang, kedua-dua sahabat melambung bersama-sama. Jarak antara kepala mereka tetap sama. Penerima mengukur jarak itu, bukan ketinggian, jadi hentakan (gangguan) tidak memberi kesan.)
↔ Swipe sideways to see the whole picture

RS-422 in short

  • 4 wires: one twisted pair to transmit, one to receive, so it is full duplex.
  • Distance: up to 1200 m (4000 ft).
  • Speed: up to 10 Mbps (on short cables).
  • Devices: one sender can drive up to 10 receivers.
  • The wires in each pair are twisted so that noise hits both equally.

3.6RS-485: the industrial workhorse

Key ideaRS-485 puts up to 32 devices on one twisted pair (multidrop). Only one may talk at a time, so the master calls each address in turn.
Analogy: a lecturer and a classThe lecturer (master) calls one student by name (address). Only that student answers. If two students shout at once, nobody understands either of them. (Pensyarah (master) memanggil seorang pelajar dengan namanya (alamat). Hanya pelajar itu yang menjawab. Jika dua pelajar menjerit serentak, tiada siapa yang faham kedua-duanya.)

    Termination resistors: stopping the echo

    On a long cable, a signal reaching the far end bounces back like an echo and spoils the next bits. A resistor of about 100 to 120 Ω (matching the cable) at each end of the bus soaks the signal up. Turn termination OFF above and poll again.

    Analogy: an empty hall versus a curtained roomShout in an empty hall and your words echo back over the next ones. Hang thick curtains at the ends of the room and the echo disappears. The termination resistors are the curtains. (Jika anda menjerit di dalam dewan kosong, suara anda bergema semula dan menindih perkataan seterusnya. Gantung langsir tebal di kedua-dua hujung bilik dan gema itu hilang. Perintang penamat ialah langsir itu.)

    RS-485 is the usual road for Modbus RTU (Chapter 2.8). Each device needs a unique address, set on the device, so that only one answers each question.

    3.8Converters

    Key ideaA converter is a bridge between two electrical standards, for example from a PC's USB or RS-232 port to an RS-485 network. It changes the signal, not the message.
    Analogy: a travel plug adapterYour Malaysian plug does not fit a Japanese socket, so you use an adapter. The electricity is the same; only the shape of the plug changes. A converter changes the wires and voltages, but the Modbus message inside stays exactly the same. (Changing the language itself needs a gateway, Chapter 2.) (Palam Malaysia anda tidak muat pada soket Jepun, jadi anda menggunakan penyesuai palam. Elektriknya sama; hanya bentuk palam yang berubah. Penukar mengubah wayar dan voltan, tetapi mesej Modbus di dalamnya kekal sama. Untuk menukar bahasa itu sendiri, kita perlukan gateway (Bab 2).)

    3.9Why we need networks

    Key ideaWiring every device directly to every other device does not scale. A shared network replaces bundles of cables with one, and lets many PLCs work as a team.

    What a network gives you

    Less wiring, less cost

    One cable can serve from two to several hundred PLCs, computers and smart devices, often daisy-chained one after another.

    A team of PLCs

    Control is spread over many PLCs (more reliable: one failure does not stop everything), but they still coordinate with each other.

    Easier fault-finding

    Programming, debugging and troubleshooting are done from one place, and operators can enter data from one terminal.

    Watch out: many PLC makers sell their own networks that do not talk to other brands (different protocols, commands, error checks and cables). See the vendor comparison in Chapter 2.3.

    3.10Network topologies

    Key ideaA topology is the shape of a network: how the cables join the nodes. The four basic shapes are bus, ring, star and tree, and each fails in a different way.

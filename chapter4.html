4.1The HMI in the loop

Key ideaThe HMI works in both directions. Input: the operator tells the machine what to do. Output: the machine tells the operator what is happening. Together they make a loop that never stops.
Analogy: driving a carYou turn the steering wheel and press the pedals (input). The dashboard shows your speed and fuel (output). You look, decide, act, and look again. (Anda memusing stereng dan menekan pedal (input). Papan pemuka menunjukkan kelajuan dan minyak (output). Anda melihat, membuat keputusan, bertindak, dan melihat semula.)
↔ Swipe sideways to see the whole picture

Input or output?

Input devices let the user tell the machine what to do: keyboards, touchscreens, joysticks and mice. Output keeps the user updated: what appears on the screen, or what the machine itself does.

HMIs you already use

An air conditioner panel and an ATM machine are both HMIs. Try the air conditioner: every button you press is an INPUT, and the display answering you is an OUTPUT.

4.2What is under the screen

Key ideaThe HMI does not drive the machine by itself. It talks to the PLC, and the PLC switches the real devices. The computer is only needed to write and download the program and the screens.

Pick an example from the notes, change the setting on the HMI, and watch the command travel down to the device.

Computer

CX-Programmer writes the PLC program; CX-Designer draws the HMI screens. Used to install and download, then it can be unplugged.

HMI

Set up parameters (temperature, speed, counts) and control the I/O devices through the PLC.

PLC and devices

The PLC runs the logic and drives the real devices: thermocouple, motor, indicator lamp.

4.3How HMIs evolved

Key ideaHMIs grew from walls of buttons and dials into touchscreens, tablets and now AI and virtual reality. We use HMIs every day on our phones and computers, and industry uses them for mass production.

4.4Where HMIs are used, and why

Key ideaA good HMI saves time. It increases productivity and uptime and keeps product quality consistent, so it is used in almost every industry.

Four advantages of an HMI

Enhances communicationOperators, engineers and managers all see the same live picture of the plant.
Improves productivityProblems are spotted and fixed quickly, so machines stop less often.
Prevents injuriesOperators control dangerous machines from a safe place instead of standing next to them.
Records dataCounts, trends and alarms are saved, so you can look back and improve.
Saves time→More uptime→Consistent quality

4.5What makes a good HMI?

Key ideaThe goal of every HMI screen is situation awareness: one look tells the operator what the process is doing and what needs attention. Five rules get you there.
Analogy: a car dashboardA good dashboard shows speed big and clear, uses a red light only for real problems, and puts everything in the same place in every car. A dashboard covered in flashing colours would be dangerous. (Papan pemuka yang baik menunjukkan kelajuan dengan besar dan jelas, menggunakan lampu merah hanya untuk masalah sebenar, dan meletakkan semuanya di tempat yang sama dalam setiap kereta. Papan pemuka yang penuh dengan warna berkelip akan berbahaya.)

4.6Rule 1: the schematic screen

Rule 1 of 5
Key ideaA schematic (mimic) screen draws the process the way it really looks: tanks, pipes, pumps and valves. If a tank's level bar is rising, it is natural to click the valve at its outlet to bring the level down.
Analogy: a map versus a list of directions"Turn left after 300 m, then right after 1.2 km…" is hard to follow. A map shows you at a glance where you are and where to go. ("Belok kiri selepas 300 m, kemudian kanan selepas 1.2 km…" sukar diikut. Peta menunjukkan dengan sekali pandang di mana anda berada dan ke mana hendak pergi.)

Try it. The tank is filling. When the HIGH alarm comes on, open the outlet valve as fast as you can. Do it once with the schematic and once with a plain list of numbers, and compare your times.

4.7Rule 2: colour

Rule 2 of 5
Key ideaChoose a calm background and save strong colours for things that need attention. If everything is colourful, the alarm cannot stand out.
Analogy: a highlighter penIf you highlight every line in your textbook, nothing stands out any more. Highlight only the key sentence and your eye goes straight to it. (Jika anda menyerlahkan setiap baris dalam buku teks, tiada apa yang menonjol lagi. Serlahkan hanya ayat yang penting dan mata anda terus tertumpu padanya.)

Choosing the background colour

The first DCS screens had a black background. Black gives good contrast with almost every other colour, but the curved CRT screens reflected light, so control rooms were kept dark. Tap each background to compare.

4.8Rule 3: layout

Rule 3 of 5
Key ideaPut things where the eye expects them: the process flows from left to right, related items sit together, and the title, menu and alarm bar stay in the same place on every screen.
Analogy: a supermarketRice is with rice, drinks are with drinks, and the cashier is always at the exit. You find things without thinking. (Beras bersama beras, minuman bersama minuman, dan juruwang sentiasa di pintu keluar. Anda menjumpai barang tanpa perlu berfikir.)

4.9Rule 4: process values and labels

Rule 4 of 5
Key ideaLive process values must stand out more (bigger, bolder) than the labels and units next to them. Values that are not critical belong on a detail screen, not the overview.
Analogy: a newspaperThe headline is big so you see it first; the details are in smaller print inside the article. (Tajuk utama dicetak besar supaya anda nampak dahulu; butirannya dalam tulisan kecil di dalam artikel.)

4.10Rule 5: consistency and navigation

Rule 5 of 5
Key ideaAn HMI is a collection of screens, not one screen. The operator must move easily between the overview and the detail screens, and every screen must use the same layout, buttons and colour meanings.
Analogy: a shopping mallEvery floor has the same signboards, and the lift buttons are always in the same place. In a mall where every floor looked different, you would get lost. (Setiap tingkat mempunyai papan tanda yang sama, dan butang lif sentiasa di tempat yang sama. Dalam pusat beli-belah yang setiap tingkatnya berbeza, anda akan sesat.)
